# The Mithras Liturgy
## Edited and Translated by Marvin W. Meyer

### Preface

This work provides the reader with a brief introduction, Greek text, and English translation of the fascinating though difficult document known as the Mithras Liturgy.

Originally prepared for a seminar on Hellenistic mystery religions at Claremont Graduate School, Fall 1975, under Dr. Hans Dieter Betz.

### Introduction

The Mithras Liturgy is part of the great magical codex of Paris (Papyrus 574 of the Bibliotheque Nationale). Key points:

1. **Dating and Context**
   - Compiled in early fourth century CE
   - Part of an Egyptian magician's working library
   - Lines 475-834 of the codex constitute the Mithras Liturgy

2. **Historical Significance**
   - Named by A. Dieterich in 1903
   - Proposed as an official liturgy of the Mithras cult
   - Represents ritual for soul's ascent and immortalization

3. **Key Elements**
   - Mention of "the great god Helios Mithras" (line 482)
   - Invocation of the elements (lines 487-537)
   - Description of fire-breathing god Aion (lines 587-616)
   - Portraits of Helios (lines 635-637)
   - Description of the highest God (lines 693-704)

4. **Scholarly Debate**
   - Some scholars (F. Cumont, R. Reitzenstein) questioned Mithraic origin
   - Suggested connections to:
     * Hermetic writings
     * Private mysteries
     * Graeco-Egyptian syncretism
     * Magic and solar piety

5. **Contemporary Understanding**
   - Represents a fringe variety of Mithraism
   - Shows Egyptian adaptation of Mithraic traditions
   - Demonstrates syncretistic nature of late ancient religion
   - Emphasizes individual spiritual practice

### Historical Context

The text represents a unique blend of:
- Traditional Mithraic elements
- Egyptian magical practices
- Hellenistic mystery traditions
- Individual spiritual ascent practices

### Significance

1. **Religious Studies**

2. **Historical Value**

3. **Ritual Practice**
